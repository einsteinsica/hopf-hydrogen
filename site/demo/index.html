What's happening

Three quarks (red, green, blue) rotate independently through a 4D sphere. The electron is what happens when you multiply all three rotations together — its path is entirely determined by quark geometry.

Right now it's tracing the 1s orbital — a perfect sphere. No formula tells it to be spherical. The shape emerges because incommensurate rotations on S³ visit every direction equally.

The trail it leaves behind is the probability cloud. Where the electron spends more time, the cloud is denser. When it glows bright, the harmonic weight is high — it's in a region the wavefunction favors. When it dims, it's crossing a node. Change the orbital to see how the same quark path produces every atomic shape.
